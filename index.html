The problem

Why aerial detection breaks ground-level models

  • 01

    Small, dense targets

    Aerial viewpoints shrink pedestrians and vehicles to a few dozen pixels, often packed dozens-deep in a single frame — the validation split alone averages roughly 70 objects per image.

  • 02

    Real-world stakes

    Traffic monitoring, crowd safety, disaster response, and infrastructure inspection all depend on detectors that work reliably from a drone's vantage point.

  • 03

    A genuine detection gap

    Detectors tuned on ground-level, large-object datasets like COCO underperform sharply here — this project trains and evaluates specifically for the aerial case.

Model: YOLO11n

Single-stage, anchor-free detector — predicts class and box directly from feature maps in one pass, with no separate region-proposal stage, which is what makes real-time speed possible.

The nano (n) variant was chosen deliberately for Colab's free-tier GPU and session time limits, initialized from COCO-pretrained weights and fine-tuned on VisDrone — transfer learning, not training from scratch.

Per-class AP@50

Reading the gap: "car" reaches 75.0% AP@50 — more than double the overall mean — while "bicycle" (8.5%) and "awning-tricycle" (10.5%) sit far below it. Both weak classes are rare in the validation set and visually thin at aerial altitude, easily confused with visually similar categories. The overall mean is pulled down by a specific subset of rare, small classes, not uniform weakness across the model.

Novelty

SAHI: Slicing Aided Hyper Inference

Recovering the small objects a single full-frame pass misses — applied at inference time, with zero retraining.

  • 1

    The problem

    A pedestrian occupying 20×20 pixels in a 2000×1500 frame gets downsampled to almost nothing by the time it reaches the network's input resolution.

  • 2

    The fix

    SAHI slices the full image into overlapping 512×512 tiles, runs detection on each tile at full resolution, then merges results with NMS to remove duplicate detections at tile edges.

  • 3

    The tradeoff

    More tiles means more forward passes per image — small-object recall should improve, but FPS drops. This is the accuracy/speed tradeoff under evaluation.
